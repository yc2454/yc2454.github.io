About

I am a PhD student in the Department of Computer Science at Rutgers University, advised by Prof. Santosh Nagarakatte in the Rutgers Architecture and Programming Languages (APL) group.

My research sits at the intersection of programming languages and operating systems. I work on making the Linux kernel's eBPF verifier accept more safe programs without growing its trusted computing base using ideas from proof-carrying code, abstract interpretation, and SMT-based proof generation.

Before starting my PhD, I spent three years as a software engineer at CertiK, building formal verification tools for smart contracts: including compilers from Solidity to Dafny and Boogie and a JML-style specification language for Solidity. I hold an MS in Computer Science from Duke University and studied biological engineering at Cornell University and Zhejiang University.

Projects

Alivio

A userspace verifier and proof generator for proof-carrying BPF. Alivio mirrors the Linux kernel's eBPF verifier in Rust — reconstructing control flow and tracking register state through abstract interpretation — and generates machine-checkable SMT proofs for the program points the kernel's own analysis cannot prove safe. It matches the kernel verifier's verdict on all 3,267 BPF selftests and produces proof bundles that let a patched kernel load production eBPF programs from Cilium, Calico, and Inspektor Gadget that the stock verifier rejects with zero userspace round trips at load time.

Verified Translator from EVM Bytecode to eWASM

A translator from a subset of Ethereum Virtual Machine bytecode to WebAssembly, with a machine-checked proof of semantic preservation in Coq. Built during my MS at Duke.
